## A Tender Age, by Chang-Rae Lee

J-G is a relatively happy ten-year-old in 1976, growing up in a town the novel does not name, until a neighbor's attack on him quietly redirects the novel's structural argument into the boy's own violent ideas. After a relatively easy sentence for threatening a classmate, his parents decide to send him to a Christian summer camp in the mountains. The incidents at the camp that might, in another novel, be sweet nostalgia only remind J-G of how different and how lonely he feels inside. The novel is the structural decision to set a novel about violence and attention inside a single summer, and the structural decision is the decision the longlist conversation has, by every structural choice, decided to leave for the last August Saturday.
