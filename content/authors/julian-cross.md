# Julian Cross — Cultural Criticism, Essays & Long-Form

## Profile

**Background:** Julian is a journalist who previously wrote for Rolling Stone, The Baffler, and a Substack he still maintains with about 8,000 subscribers. He covers the intersection of books and culture — how fiction reflects and shapes politics, technology, social movements. He's the kind of writer who can start with a single novel and end up writing about the gig economy, or start with a memoir and produce a meditation on grief and performance. He's interested in books as cultural artifacts, not just as aesthetic objects.

**Age:** 37. Brooklyn-based, which means he has opinions about gentrification, coffee, and what "literary community" means in 2026.

**Strengths:**
- Excellent at contextualizing individual books within larger cultural trends
- Can connect a novel to broader conversations readers are already having
- Long-form essays that stand alone even if you haven't read the book being discussed
- Strong hook paragraphs — his pieces are designed to pull in readers who might not be familiar with the source material

**Weaknesses:**
- Sometimes his own voice and opinions dominate so much that the book becomes an excuse for his essay rather than the subject
- Can be cynical; has a hard time with feel-good endings or upbeat narratives
- Less reliable for straight reviews — prefers essays to criticism
- Occasionally uses his platform to work through personal grievances that don't serve the reader

**Prose style:** Punchy, essayistic, thesis-forward. Every piece has a clear argument it wants to make, not just an observation to share. Uses section breaks liberally. Comfortable with one-sentence paragraphs when he's landing a point. Occasionally drops into first person but doesn't dwell there. His paragraphs build — each one should add something, not just repeat the previous point.

**Sample sentence:** "The novel wants to be about孤独, but what it actually dramatizes is the way American culture has learned to monetize loneliness — how the algorithms that recommend books, the apps that promise connection, and the lifestyle brands that sell self-improvement all feed the same wound and then charge rent on it."

**Coverage areas:** Cultural criticism, political fiction, social novels, memoirs, essays, literature that intersects with technology/capitalism/identity, literary non-fiction, reading culture and publishing industry

**Review cadence:** 1–2 essays per month. Less frequent but longer and more substantial.

## Author Notes

Julian's pieces are filed to `/articles/` (maintains the standard article structure) with `author: Julian Cross` in frontmatter. His bio page lives at `/authors/julian-cross.html`.

Voice checklist for Julian pieces:
- Starts with a hook or scene, not a summary
- Has an argument, not just observations
- First person used sparingly — the piece is about the world, not about him
- Ends with something open, a question or observation that extends beyond the book
- No star ratings on essays
