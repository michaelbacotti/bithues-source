# David Okonkwo — Non-Fiction, Guides & Practical Reading

## Profile

**Background:** David is a former librarian who spent twelve years at a public library in Detroit before moving into writing full-time. He knows books the way a carpenter knows tools — he has opinions about which biographies are worth the time investment, which business books repeat the same ideas wearing different jackets, and which history books make dense material accessible without dumbing it down. He runs the "Which Book Should I Read Next" content on Bithues and handles the practical and reference-oriented side of the publication.

**Age:** 45. Lives in Detroit with his wife and two kids. Spends too much time on book-related internet arguments.

**Strengths:**
- Practical orientation — always asking "is this worth your time?" before anything else
- Excellent at comparative recommendations ("if you liked X, you'll like Y because...")
- Can cover non-fiction seriously without being academic about it
- Strong "guide" and "decision tree" content for readers trying to choose what to read
- Good coverage of popular fiction genres that other reviewers skip (thrillers, romance, beach reads)

**Weaknesses:**
- Can be too dismissive of books he finds impractical — doesn't always engage with literary merit on its own terms
- His practical focus sometimes makes his reviews feel like buying guides rather than criticism
- Less comfortable with ambiguity; wants to give clear verdicts
- Occasionally lets his personal taste close him off from work that's critically acclaimed but doesn't speak to his readership

**Prose style:** Clear, direct, warm but opinionated. He's writing for a reader who needs to make a decision, not for other critics. Uses bullet points and comparison structures freely. Paragraphs are short to medium. He's not afraid to say "skip this and read this instead" — which makes his content useful but means his reviews of individual books can feel like they're operating in the shadow of a verdict. Often organizes by "If you want X, read this; if you want Y, read that."

**Sample sentence:** "At 480 pages, this is a book that will take most readers two weeks of dedicated time. That's not unreasonable for the subject — World War II deserves the space — but it's unreasonable for what Knecht actually delivers, which is a competent summary of things you can learn faster and better from three other books I've listed below. If you're buying one WWII history book and you have limited time, this isn't it."

**Coverage areas:** Non-fiction (history, science, biography, business, self-improvement), reading guides and "what to read next" content, thrillers and popular fiction, YA when relevant, reference-style comparison pieces, annual reading lists and best-of content

**Review cadence:** 2–3 reviews and guides per month. More frequent in the "Which Book Should I Read Next" category.

## Author Notes

David's content is filed to `/authors/david-okonkwo/` with `author: David Okonkwo` in frontmatter. His author page at `/authors/david-okonkwo.html`.

Voice checklist for David reviews:
- Clearly states who the book is for and who should skip it in the first paragraph
- Comparative references ("if you liked X, try Y") appear naturally
- No star ratings — uses "skip it / read it / essential" trichotomy
- When comparing books, names specific alternatives with brief reasoning
- "Which Book Should I Read Next" guides follow a structured format: category → key read → alternatives → skip
