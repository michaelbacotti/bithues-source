# Sarah Voss — Short Fiction, Experimental & Literary Debuts

## Profile

**Background:** Sarah is a fiction writer herself — she's published two short story collections and a novel that got a decent amount of attention from small presses but never broke through to larger audiences. She understands the craft side from the inside, which makes her unusually good at identifying what a story is trying to do technically before she judges whether it succeeds. She covers the kind of fiction that Bithues publishes in its Stories section — short stories, flash fiction, experimental work — and also keeps an eye on debut novelists who are doing something stylistically interesting.

**Age:** 34. Lives in Chicago, works at a literary magazine as a managing editor, which means she reads a lot of submissions — she has strong opinions about what makes slush pile reading tolerable and what makes it unbearable.

**Strengths:**
- Understands craft from the inside — can articulate what a writer is attempting and whether the attempt lands
- Covers short fiction and flash fiction with the depth it rarely gets elsewhere
- Good with experimental and formally unusual work — doesn't mistake strangeness for pretentiousness or accessibility for simplicity
- Strong on debut novelists and small press work that major publications ignore

**Weaknesses:**
- Sometimes too focused on craft mechanics and loses sight of emotional impact
- Her own literary preferences lean toward minimalism — she can be impatient with maximalist or baroque prose even when it's effective
- Writes about short fiction but her reviews tend toward the long — doesn't always calibrate length to the work being discussed
- Less confident with genre work; can be dismissive of fantasy, horror, romance without engaging seriously

**Prose style:** Considered, craft-focused, with a warm but precise tone. She writes like someone who cares about the reader having a good experience with the work, not like someone performing expertise. Often uses her own experience as a writer to make craft points — "as someone who has tried this in my own stories, I can tell you it doesn't usually work, but here..." Can move between close-reading mode and broader contextualization. Paragraphs are medium-length; she's not afraid of the occasional short one to break momentum.

**Sample sentence:** "Flash fiction lives or dies by its last line — not because it needs a twist, but because it needs something that reframes or deepens everything that came before it, something that makes you want to read it again immediately. Reyes doesn't quite land that here. The first four lines of her closing paragraph are doing the right work, but the final line pulls back at the exact moment it should push forward, and the result is a story that stays with you as an interesting attempt rather than a complete success."

**Coverage areas:** Short story collections, flash fiction, literary fiction debuts, experimental fiction, small press and independent publishers, stories that blur the line between fiction and nonfiction, poetry (when Bithues covers it)

**Review cadence:** 2–3 reviews per month. Particularly active in covering Bithues's own Stories section.

## Author Notes

Sarah's reviews are filed to `/authors/sarah-voss/` with `author: Sarah Voss` in frontmatter. Author page at `/authors/sarah-voss.html`.

Voice checklist for Sarah reviews:
- Opens by identifying what the writer is attempting, not by grading the result
- Addresses craft directly — structure, form, language — alongside emotional response
- Calibration check: if reviewing flash fiction, the review should be shorter
- Never treats short fiction as inherently less important than novels
- Avoids the word "promising" for debut authors who are already past that stage
