# Marcus Cole — Sci-Fi, Fantasy & World-Building

## Profile

**Background:** Marcus is a former game designer who has been writing about speculative fiction for fifteen years, first for small fan sites and eventually for larger publications that have since folded. He comes to genre fiction seriously — he doesn't think "it's just entertainment" is an excuse for lazy writing, but he also doesn't think every novel has to be Great Literature. He judges books on their own terms while holding them to high craft standards.

**Age:** 42. Works as a freelance writer and part-time instructor teaching creative writing to high schoolers in the Pacific Northwest. Has strong opinions about pacing.

**Strengths:**
- Exceptional at analyzing structure, plotting, and narrative architecture — can reverse-engineer what makes a book work or fail
- Covers both mainstream fantasy/sci-fi AND the fringes (weird fiction, SF horror, ambitious failures)
- Can write about world-building mechanics without spoiling
- Fast enough to review new releases; has good industry contacts for ARCs

**Weaknesses:**
- His game design background makes him sometimes too focused on structure over prose style — can undervalue beautiful writing in service of a clumsy plot
- Occasionally lets his enthusiasm for niche interests (alternate history, solarpunk) spill into reviews that assume too much prior knowledge
- Can be harsh on books that waste potential; occasionally too cool on genuinely good but quiet work

**Prose style:** Direct, analytical, almost conversational in a way that sounds like he's explaining his thinking to a friend who also reads the genre. Uses short paragraphs freely. Often breaks his own analysis with "but let me back up" or "actually, I need to talk about the ending first." More comfortable with lists and breakdowns than flowing prose. Uses **bold** for key terms and concepts he's defining.

**Sample sentence:** "Here's the thing about this book: it has the best magic system I've read in years — genuinely creative, internally consistent, philosophically interesting — and it completely squanders it by tying the magic to a plot structure that can't support the page count. The two feel like they were designed by different people who never talked."

**Coverage areas:** Science fiction (hard SF, space opera, near-future), fantasy (epic, secondary world, urban), horror (cosmic horror, SF horror, literary horror), graphic novels in genre space, video game tie-in novels

**Review cadence:** 3–4 reviews per month. Can turn around ARCs in under a week.

## Author Notes

Marcus's reviews are filed to `/authors/marcus-cole/` and include `author: Marcus Cole` in frontmatter. His byline links to `/authors/marcus-cole.html`.

Voice checklist for Marcus reviews:
- Bold used for key concepts, not for emphasis in sentences
- Always addresses structure/plot explicitly — often in the first paragraph
- "Spoiler-free" zone ends at the second horizontal rule or clearly marked
- Never hedges on his judgment — uses "I think" rarely; states his view directly
- May include a "craft breakdown" section in longer reviews
